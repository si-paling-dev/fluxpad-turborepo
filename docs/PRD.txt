### 2. Fair Launch Auction

In the world of crypto, a **Fair Launch Auction (FLA)** is a price-discovery mechanism designed to prevent "whales" (large investors) and bots from snatching up all the tokens at a fixed low price before the general public has a chance to participate. Instead of a fixed price, the price starts high and gradually decreases over time, or adjusts based on demand.

A fair launch auction addresses three major problems in token launches:

- **No Gas Wars:** Since the price starts high and drops slowly, there is no incentive for everyone to spam the network at the exact same second. You simply wait until the price hits your target.
- **Bot Resistance:** Bots usually profit by buying low-priced tokens instantly and dumping them. If the starting price is high, the "easy profit" margin disappears.
- **Equal Access:** It allows individual investors to compete with venture capitalists. The price is determined by collective market demand, not a backroom deal.

The most common form of a fair launch auction is the **Dutch Auction** or a **Liquidity Bootstrapping Pool (LBP)**. Here is the typical flow:

- **The Ceiling Start:** The auction begins at a deliberately high "ceiling" price (e.g., **$10**), which is usually higher than what the market is expected to pay.
- **The Decay:** If no one buys, the price automatically drops according to a pre-set mathematical formula ($P(t)$ decreases as time $t$ increases)
- **The Discovery:** As the price drops, it eventually hits a level that a buyer finds attractive. When they buy, it signals the market value.
- **The Settlement:** In many modern fair launches, everyone pays the **same final clearing price**. If you bid early at **$5** and the auction ends at **$2**, you are refunded the difference or given more tokens to match the **$2** rate.

The Dutch Auction is the best first step because it is **self-contained**. You can write the entire logic in a single smart contract without needing to integrate with other complex protocols. **What you'll learn:**

- **Time-Based Logic:** How to use `block.timestamp` to create a price that decays linearly or exponentially. $Price = P_{start} - (rate \times time)$
- **Settlement Logic:** Handling a "Clearing Price" where every participant pays the same final price, requiring you to manage refunds or varying token allocations.
- **State Management:** Tracking bids and ensuring the contract doesn't "break" if no one buys.

### 3. High-level Architecture Implementation

Starting with the **Dutch Auction**, we’ll focus on the "Clearing Price" model. This is the gold standard for fairness: everyone from the first bidder to the last pays the exact same price—the lowest price reached before the tokens sold out.

Here is the high-level architecture for building this on Monad.

**Phase 1: The Auction Parameters**

Before the code runs, you define the "Shape" of the auction. You need four primary variables:

1. **Start Price ($P_{start}$):** A high "ceiling" to deter immediate bot snipes.
2. **Reserve Price ($P_{min}$):** The lowest price you’re willing to accept.
3. **Duration:** How long the decay takes (e.g., 24 hours).
4. **Quantity:** Total tokens for sale.

**Phase 2: The Smart Contract Logic**

You can break the contract into three main functions. Because Monad is parallelized, you want to keep these functions "clean" to avoid unnecessary state locks.

- **The Price Calculator (Read-Only)**
    
    This function calculates the current price based on the elapsed time.
    
    - **The Math:** $CurrentPrice = P_{start} - \frac{(P_{start} - P_{min}) \times (CurrentTime - StartTime)}{Duration}$
    - **Monad Tip:** Since this is a `view` function, it’s instant and free for frontends to call.

---

- **The Commit Function (Write)**
    
    Users don't "buy" tokens immediately; they "commit" capital (e.g., $MON$ or $USDC$).
    
    - The contract records: `TotalCapitalCommitted += UserDeposit`.
    - The contract checks: "Is the auction still active?”
    - **The Logic:** You don't give them tokens yet because you don't know the final price. You just log their contribution.

---

- **The Settlement (The Finish Line)**
    
    Once the time is up or the "implied" quantity is sold:
    
    - **Calculate Clearing Price:** Based on the total capital raised vs. tokens available.
    - **Distribution:** Users call a `claim()` function.
    - **Refunds:** If a user committed **$100** when the price was **$10**, but the final price was **$5**, they get their tokens plus any leftover change (or just more tokens, depending on your model).

**Phase 3: The Workflow (User Journey)**

| **Step** | **Action**  | **What happens on Monad** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Deployment | You deploy the contract. Monad’s low fees make this negligible. |
| 2 | The Decay | The price drops every second. Users watch the UI update in real-time. |
| 3 | Bidding | Users send funds. Parallel execution allows hundreds of bids per second without "clogging" the pipe. |
| 4 | Closing | The auction hits the time limit or the $P_{min}$. |
| 5 | Claiming | Users trigger a transaction to pull their tokens and $MON$ refunds to their wallets |

**Phase 4: Implementation Checklist for You**

- **Define the Decay Curve:** Will it be **Linear** (straight line) or **Exponential** (drops fast at first, then slows down)? Exponential is usually better for finding "true" market value.
- **Safety Guards:** Use `ReentrancyGuard`. Even though Monad is fast, standard EVM vulnerabilities still apply.
- **The "Sold Out" Trigger:** Decide if the auction ends early if the committed capital at the current price exceeds the total supply.
